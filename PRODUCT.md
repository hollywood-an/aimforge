# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Competitive FPS players — Valorant, CS2, Apex, Overwatch — who warm up before ranked sessions or grind aim mechanics between matches. They already know Aim Lab and Kovaak's; many use them daily. Context: sitting at a gaming setup, mouse in hand, usually with 5–20 minutes to spend, often mid-warmup where every second in menus is a second not training. A secondary audience is newer FPS players who found "aim trainer" through a friend or a video and need the first run to explain itself.

## Product Purpose

AimForge is a free, no-install browser aim trainer: 14 scenarios across flicking, precision, tracking, switching and reflex, plus a custom drill builder and a six-stage ranked benchmark. It exists to make serious aim practice instant — no download, no launcher, no account — while keeping the things that make desktop trainers credible: raw input, true cross-game sensitivity conversion, and honest scoring with persistent progression. Success looks like a player finishing a benchmark, getting a rank they believe, and coming back tomorrow to beat it.

## Positioning

Desktop-trainer credibility at browser-tab speed: click a link, and eight seconds later you're training with your exact in-game sensitivity.

## Brand Personality

Precise, competitive, respectful of the player's time. The tone of a good coach: direct, numbers-first, quietly encouraging — never carnival, never patronizing. Emotionally the app should produce focus during runs and earned pride at the results screen; hype lives in the player's performance, not in the chrome around it.

## Anti-references

- The generic "neon gamer" aesthetic: RGB gradients everywhere, aggressive glows on every element, HUD clutter. AimForge is dark and competitive but disciplined — glow is reserved for moments (rank reveal, new PB), not ambience.
- Free-to-play mobile-game energy: coins, spinning badges, confetti on every action, "AMAZING!!!" toasts.
- Sterile SaaS dashboard: the opposite failure. This is a game — the results screen is a payoff, not a report.

## Design Principles

1. **The run is sacred.** Nothing may cost input latency, frame rate, or attention during play. HUD elements earn their pixels; motion never delays a resume or restart.
2. **Numbers players can trust.** Scores, ranks, and sensitivity math are displayed precisely and honestly — no rounding theater, no inflated praise. Trust is the product.
3. **Fast paths for regulars, guidance for first-timers.** A veteran restarts a run in one keypress; a newcomer always knows what to click next. Neither pays for the other.
4. **Delight at the peaks.** Celebration concentrates where it's earned — new PB, rank reveal, benchmark finish. Everything routine stays quiet so the peaks read.
5. **Earned familiarity.** Settings, tables, and forms behave like the best tools players know. Novelty is spent on the arena and the payoff moments, not on scrollbars and form controls.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative (crossfade or instant).
- Text contrast meets WCAG AA (≥4.5:1 body, ≥3:1 large text) on all surfaces.
- Full keyboard operability outside pointer-locked play: visible focus states, logical tab order, standard shortcuts (R restart, Q quit, Esc pause) documented in the UI.
- Target color is user-configurable (color-blind players pick what reads for them); crosshair fully customizable.
